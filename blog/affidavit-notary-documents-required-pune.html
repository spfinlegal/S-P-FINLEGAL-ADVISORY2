Affidavit and Notary Attestation in Pune: Types, Documents and Process

Written by Adv. Poonam Shinde
Statutory Notary Reference

In short

An affidavit is a written statement of facts that you swear is true. A notary checks your identity, sees you sign, and puts a seal. Bring original ID proofs, the draft or details, and keep the correct stamp paper if the office asking for it requires one.

1. What is an affidavit?

An affidavit is a written statement made on oath or solemn affirmation. For example, you may state that a name is spelt differently on two documents or that you lost an original paper. Giving false information in an affidavit is a punishable offence, so write only what is true.

2. What does a notary actually do?

  • Checks the identity of the person who signs.
  • Sees the person sign and confirms that they understood the paper.
  • Records the act in the notary register and puts a seal and signature.

3. What a notary does not do

  • A notary does not check whether the content is legally right or fair.
  • A notarised paper is not the same as a registered document. Sale deeds and long-term leases need registration (see our guide on notarised vs registered agreements).
  • A notary cannot attest a document that is blank or incomplete.

4. Common types of affidavits

  • Name change or name spelling correction
  • Date of birth correction
  • Address proof or residence affidavit
  • Lost document (such as marksheet or share certificate) along with the police complaint or newspaper notice where needed
  • Gap in education or employment
  • Income affidavit
  • Affidavit for passport, visa or bank use
  • Single status or marriage related declaration
  • Indemnity bond or no-objection affidavit

5. What to carry

  • Original photo ID and address proof (Aadhaar, PAN, passport, etc.)
  • Passport size photographs if needed
  • The details of facts to be written, or your draft
  • Copies of the documents you refer to in the affidavit
  • Stamp paper of the value required by the office or authority that will accept it

6. Process in 5 steps

  1. Tell us the purpose and the office where you will submit it.
  2. We prepare or review the draft with you.
  3. Arrange stamp paper if required.
  4. You sign in front of the notary with your ID.
  5. The notary attests, records it in the register and gives you the document.

7. Tips to avoid rejection

  • Ask the receiving office about the format and stamp paper value before preparing.
  • Check the spelling of all names and dates against your ID.
  • Do not use correction fluid or overwriting.
  • Keep two or three copies.

8. Name change in your documents

A name change usually needs more than an affidavit. Many people also publish a notice in a newspaper and a Government Gazette notification so that the new name is accepted everywhere. The steps can differ by department, so check the rules before you start.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between an affidavit and a notarised document?

An affidavit is a sworn written statement. Notarisation is the act of a notary verifying the identity and signature of the person.

Do I need stamp paper for every affidavit?

Not always. It depends on the purpose and the office that will accept the affidavit. Ask them first.

Can a notary make my agreement legally registered?

No. A notary only attests signatures. Documents that law requires to be registered must be registered with the Sub-Registrar.

This guide is for general information. Laws, rates and due dates change, so please confirm the latest rules or speak to us before you act.
