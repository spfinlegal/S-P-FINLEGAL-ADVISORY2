Proprietorship vs. Partnership vs. LLP vs. Pvt Ltd: Which Business Structure Should You Choose in India?

Co-Authored by Shailesh Shinde & Adv. Poonam Shinde
Practical Entity Guide
MCA & Income Tax Framework

The Golden Rule of Entity Choice

There is no single "best" entity. If you are a solo consultant or single retail shop owner, a Sole Proprietorship minimizes compliance costs. If you have 2 or more partners and want personal liability protection without high corporate audit overheads, a Limited Liability Partnership (LLP) is ideal. If you plan to raise equity funding from investors or issue ESOPs, a Private Limited Company is legally compulsory.

2. Detailed Breakdown of Each Structure

A. Sole Proprietorship

How to Start: Obtained via Maharashtra Shop & Establishment Act (Gumasta License), Udyam MSME certificate, and a Current Bank Account opened with a GST or Shop Act document.
Best For: Small retailers, local vendors, individual consultants, and freelancers testing early business ideas. Low annual filing costs, but high risk because personal assets are legally tied to business debts.

B. Partnership Firm (Indian Partnership Act, 1932)

How to Start: Draft a comprehensive Partnership Deed on stamp paper, notarize it or register it with the Registrar of Firms (ROF Maharashtra), and apply for a separate firm PAN card.
Caution: Partners have unlimited joint and several liability. If one partner incurs debt on behalf of the firm, other partners' personal assets can be held liable.

C. Limited Liability Partnership - LLP (LLP Act, 2008)

How to Start: Obtain Digital Signature Certificates (DSC), file FiLLiP form on MCA portal, draft and file Form 3 (LLP Agreement) within 30 days of incorporation.
Best For: Professional consultancies, software service agencies, doctors, and family-owned businesses who want legal asset protection without the burdensome compliance of board meetings and statutory secretarial filings.

D. Private Limited Company (Companies Act, 2013)

How to Start: SPICe+ MCA application, RUN name approval, drafting MOA & AOA, obtaining Certificate of Incorporation, PAN, TAN, and EPFO/ESIC.
Best For: Scalable startups, manufacturing businesses, and companies seeking venture capital funding, bank loan syndications, or planning long-term institutional expansion.
