Bookkeeping for Small Business in India: A Simple Guide for Beginners

Written by Shailesh Shinde
Small Business Accounting Reference

In short

Bookkeeping means writing down every rupee that comes in and goes out of your business. Good books help you file taxes correctly, get loans easily and know if you are really making a profit. Start simple and be regular.

1. What is bookkeeping?

Bookkeeping is recording your daily business transactions: sales, purchases, expenses, bank entries and payments. Accounting uses these records to prepare reports like the profit and loss statement and balance sheet.

2. Why does it matter?

  • Taxes: ITR, GST and TDS all depend on correct numbers.
  • Loans: banks ask for 2 to 3 years of financial statements.
  • Decisions: you can see which product or customer makes money.
  • Peace of mind: fewer notices and fewer last-minute problems.

3. Records every business should keep

  • Sales invoices and purchase bills
  • Bank statements and cash book
  • Expense bills and receipts
  • Payroll and salary records
  • Fixed asset list (computer, machines, vehicles)
  • Loan statements
  • GST, TDS and income tax payment challans

4. A simple routine

WhenWhat to do
DailyRecord sales, purchases, cash received and paid
WeeklyFollow up on unpaid customer invoices; check pending supplier payments
MonthlyMatch bank statement with books, file GST and TDS, check profit
YearlyPrepare final accounts, file ITR, plan advance tax

5. Cash versus accrual: what is the difference?

In cash accounting you record income when you receive money. In accrual accounting you record income when you make the sale, even if payment comes later. Companies and many businesses must follow accrual. Ask your accountant which method applies to you.

6. Keep personal and business money separate

Open a separate bank account for the business. Do not pay personal bills from it. If you take money for yourself, record it as drawings or salary. This one habit makes your books much cleaner.

7. How long should you keep records?

Income tax law generally expects business books to be kept for at least 6 years. GST law generally expects records for 72 months from the due date of the annual return. Keeping everything for 8 years is a safe habit.

8. Common mistakes

  • Mixing personal and business expenses
  • Not collecting bills for cash expenses
  • Doing the books only once a year
  • Ignoring unpaid customer invoices
  • Not reconciling bank statements

Frequently asked questions

Do I need an accountant for a small business?

It is not mandatory for every business, but a professional helps you stay compliant and avoid costly errors, especially for GST and TDS.

How long should I keep accounting records?

Income tax rules generally require at least 6 years and GST rules generally require 72 months. Keeping records for 8 years is a safe practice.
